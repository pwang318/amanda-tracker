Your daily home base. "Start Here" tells you the one thing that matters most right now, ranked across everything. Activity Guide

    
The role comes down to two things: bring in new agents, and keep the agents we have engaged and getting value from us. Every activity type below fits into one of those two buckets. Use this anytime you're not sure what to log something as.

    

      If you're mid-call and not sure which type fits, log your best guess and keep going, don't stop the conversation to check this guide. You can always fix it after: open Activity Feed, find the entry, hit Edit.
    


    

      Quick note on "Agent Talk-to's" Every touch should leave the agent with something of value: a market insight, useful feedback, a resource, a pipeline review, a file update, a genuine check-in on how their business is going. Think account executive: proactive, consultative, and always bringing something of value. It's what keeps you top-of-mind so agents think of NFM first.
    


    
1 · New Business (Growth) 🌱

    
Agents you've never worked with before. These don't count toward your Talk-to goal; they've got their own goals to hit.

    

      
Cold CallsYour very first reach-out to someone new, not in the book yet. If you've talked to them before, even once, it's not cold anymore.

      
New Realtor MeetingThe first real sit-down: go over standards of service and ask for the opportunity to earn their business. First meetings should be coffee or in-office — office tour, meet the team, go over what we offer. Save lunch for break breads with agents you already have a relationship with.

    


    
2 · Get the Opportunity 🚀

    
That first meeting isn't the finish line; they're not really on board until they send us a lead. Getting them set up on the tools is what makes that first lead possible, so keep following up until it happens.

    

      
New Agent Follow-up counts as Talk-toChecking in and helping a newly met agent get set up on HomeBot, ListReports, and Total Expert, moving them toward that first lead.

      
Onboarding checklistThe 7 quick tasks (Retained Report, Sa Team email, HomeBot, ListReports, Total Expert, group text, asking for the opportunity + referral language + LP CC'd). Complete these the same day as the meeting.

      
Gifting campaignEvery agent in this program needs a call once a week for their first 4 weeks. This is one of the most important parts of the role. Tracked on Snapshot, which follows the Ascend 4-Week Conversion Playbook: break the ice, a value touch, an opportunity call, then accountability follow-up.

    


    
3 · If They Go Quiet: Reconnect 🔁

    
If an agent hasn't sent us a lead in 90 days, they fall off and land on the Reconnect list (its own tab). That's a flag that it's time to work on earning a lead from them again. Re-engaging someone from that list is not a Cold Call: the relationship already exists, you're just picking it back up. Log it as Reconnect Outreach, not a regular Agent Talk-to, since technically they're not active until they send a lead again. It still counts toward your Talk-to goal.


    
4 · Staying in Touch 💛

    
Everything marked counts as Talk-to feeds into your weekly Talk-to goal (100/week). Log it once and it does double duty. Whatever the reason for the touch, use it to get real feedback on how the relationship is going. Every touch is an opportunity.

    

      
Agent Talk-to counts as Talk-toA proactive check-in with an agent who's currently active and sending business.

      
Reconnect Outreach counts as Talk-toThe right type for re-engaging someone from the Reconnect list. It's not a Cold Call since you already know them, but a regular Agent Talk-to isn't quite right either since they haven't sent a lead in 90+ days.

      
CTC Call counts as Talk-toProcess: call the agent, leave a voicemail if no answer, then follow up with a text. Script is in Scripts. Get feedback from both the buyer's agent and the listing agent. Share that feedback on WhatsApp with the team in real-time, proactively, the way Daniel does, proactively. Check Encompass for the CTC milestone  Always ask the listing agent for a meeting if they don't already work with us; a closing is a natural door-opener. If there's a closing issue or poor feedback, set the outcome to "Needs manager attention" so it surfaces for the team.

      
Under Contract Call counts as Talk-to, note requiredFirst call to the listing agent as soon as a deal you're the buyer's lender on goes under contract. Script is in Scripts.

      
Birthday / Holiday Outreach counts as Talk-toA personal touch: wish the agent a happy birthday or happy holidays. When the Calendar tab shows a birthday alert, send a text, give a quick call, or both. If the agent is a top producer, let the team know so they can wish them well too. When you are on a call with any agent, check if their birthday is on file — a lot of data is missing. If you do not have it, ask and add it to their contact so you never miss it.

      
Agent Appreciation counts as Talk-toRecognizing a top producer or celebrating a milestone.

      
Market Update counts as Talk-toSharing what's happening in the market: inventory, days on market, buyer demand, listing trends. This isn't financing or rates; that stays with the LP team.

      
Break Breads note required, 10+ wordsMore than a meal: sitting down with the agent to get real feedback and confirm the relationship is genuinely solid. Its own goal (5/week), separate from Talk-to's. To figure out who's due, check the Break Bread Due List on Snapshot (or the Face Time Tracker on the LP tracker), it shows last touch date per agent across both you and the LPs. Consider bringing the assigned LP along when it makes sense.

      
Realtor Buddy Referral AskAsk an existing happy agent to introduce you to someone else they know — you're not calling a new person here, you're asking the buddy to make the intro. Own goal (3/week). When the intro leads to an actual new cold call or meeting, tag it with "Referred by" on that new entry so the ask's real result gets tracked, not just that you asked.

      
Co-hosted EventHosting something together with the agent.

Agent Strategy

    
From Ascend Coaching. Talking points and frameworks for actual agent conversations.


    
The How to Refer System

    
Don't just ask for leads. Install the referral language. The agent needs the exact text, email path, and buyer expectations, not a vague ask.

    

      Agent uses the exact script at the first buyer interaction → Buyer expects a lender call within hours → Lender receives name, phone, email, goals, timing, and obstacles.
    

    

      Coach this line after sending the guide: "This is the exact language I recommend using whenever a buyer mentions they may purchase."
    


    
Coach Agents to Refer Earlier

    
Earlier introductions create more control and more conversion. Don't wait until the buyer is ready to tour homes.

    

      
Early = plan + education + excitement + lender preference

      
Late = rate comparison + urgency + weaker influence

    


    
Existing Partner Lead Growth Framework

    
Earn more share by tying support to the agent's goals. The ask doesn't feel like begging when it follows value, feedback, and accountability.

    

      
1. State the goal"I want to earn all of your business."

      
2. Ask for feedback"What can I improve to earn more?"

      
3. Learn their goalsTransactions, pipeline, stuck buyers.

      
4. Offer a specific actionReview stalled buyers, events, intros.

      
5. Ask for greater opportunityEarlier intros, larger lead share.

    

    

      Core question: "What can I improve to earn more of your business?"
    


    
Relationship Builder vs. Transactional Lender

    
Deep relationships reduce partner turnover. Business relationships are replaceable; personal loyalty is harder to displace.

    

      
Transactional lender: calls only when there's a deal, sends generic marketing, asks for leads, disappears after closing.

      
Relationship builder: builds a personal cadence, creates value, helps the agent hit goals, puts the agent first.

    

    
Relationship actions: relevant humor, family milestones, events with partners, agent introductions.

Under Contract Call (Listing Agent)

    
Call the listing agent as soon as a deal you're the buyer's lender on goes under contract. Set the tone early, this is your first real touchpoint with them on this file.

    

      "Hey [Agent Name], this is [Your Name] from the Daniel Sa Team at NFM Lending. I'm calling about your listing at [Property Address], which has just gone into contract. I'm the lender representing the buyer. First, I have a couple of questions about the transaction, I want to make sure we have a smooth process. Does that sound good? Congratulations again on the sale."
    

    
Questions to cover:

    
    
      
  • Any special accommodations for your seller, a POA or mail-away closing, we should know about?
    • 
      
  • Will your seller use the sale proceeds to purchase another home?
    • 
      
  • Will this be a simultaneous closing?
        
      
          
    • Would it help to get the final closing package and closing disclosure three full days in advance?
      • 
          
    • Do you know which title company will handle your seller's next purchase?
      • 
        
    
      
    • 
      
  • Do you have a transaction coordinator we should keep updated throughout?
    • 
    

    

      "Our team is available nights and weekends, so I'm going to send you a quick text with my contact info. Please don't hesitate to reach out anytime if you or your seller need anything."
    

    
Close for the meeting before ending the call.

    
After the call, send a follow-up text with your contact info. Log this as an Under Contract Call — it counts as a Talk-to.


    
Under Contract Call (Buyer's Agent)

    
Call the buyer's agent once the file goes under contract. This call sets the tone for how you will work together throughout.

    

      "Hey [Agent Name], this is [Your Name] from the Daniel Sa Team at NFM Lending. I'm calling about [Buyer Name]'s contract on [Property Address] — congratulations to you and your buyers for going into contract! I wanted to reach out personally and introduce myself. [LP Name] will be your main point of contact throughout the transaction and our team is here to support you every step of the way."
    

    
Questions to cover:

    
    
      
  • Are there any concerns or anything we should know going in?
    • 
      
  • What's the best way to keep you updated throughout the process — text, email, calls?
    • 
      
  • Regarding the closing date — we want to make sure we stick with the contracted date. We do not move up closing dates. Once we are clear to close, if all parties are in agreement we can revisit at that point.
    • 
    

    

      "Our team is available nights and weekends, so I'm going to send you a quick text with my contact info. Please reach out anytime. We are going to take good care of your buyer."
    

    
If they don't already work with us regularly, close for a meeting before ending the call: "I'd love to sit down sometime, even just 15 minutes, to see if it makes sense to work together."

    
After the call, send a follow-up text with your contact info. Log this as an Under Contract Call — it counts as a Talk-to.


    
CTC Call

    
Process: call the agent, leave a voicemail if no answer, follow up with a text if still no response.

    

      "Hi [Agent Name], this is [Your Name], I'm the Relationship Manager for the Daniel Sa Team at NFM Lending. Congrats on getting [Buyer/Property] to clear to close! I wanted to check in personally, was there anything that went really smoothly for you, or anything we could've done better on our end?"
    

    
If it's the listing agent and they don't already work with us, bridge straight into the meeting ask before ending the call:

    

      "I'd love to actually sit down and meet sometime, even just 15 minutes, to see if it makes sense to work together."
    


    
Cold Call — Opening Script

    
Before you dial: Pull up RETR and look them up. Find a recent sale or listing and mention it in your opener — "I saw your listing at [address] just closed, congrats!" It makes the call personal and shows you did your homework.

    
Goal: introduce yourself, explain how we support agents, and schedule a 20-minute meeting.

    

      "Hey [Agent Name], I wanted to reach out and introduce myself. We love working alongside agents to help buyers better understand their financing options, create listing-specific payment strategies, and explore creative financing options to help ensure you and your clients are winning in this market. I would love to learn more about your business and explore ways we can support you. Would you be open to a quick 20-minute meeting sometime next week?"
    

    
When scheduling — offer two options:

    

      "Would Tuesday at 11:00 or Thursday at 2:00 work better for you?"
    


    
Cold Call — Common Responses

    

      
What's the meeting about?"We'd love to learn more about the clients and areas you serve, what you're currently focused on growing, and where additional lending support may be helpful. We can also share some of the financing strategies and listing resources we provide to our agent partners."

      
Already have a lender"That's completely understandable, and we're not asking you to replace a relationship that's working well. We'd simply love the opportunity to introduce ourselves and become an additional resource when you need a second opinion, have a challenging scenario, or need extra support. Would you still be open to a 20-minute meeting?"

      
Send me some info"Absolutely. I want to make sure I send something relevant. Would buyer financing options, listing-specific payment strategies, or solutions for more challenging scenarios be most helpful? I'll send that over today. Would it be okay if I follow up with you on [day]?"

      
I'm busy right now"No problem at all, I completely understand. Would there be a better time for me to call you back, or would it be easier if I sent you a quick text?"

      
Not interested"I completely understand. Thank you for taking my call. I'll send you my contact information in case you ever need an additional lending resource or a second opinion on a scenario."

    


    
No Answer — Follow-Up Text

    
Send immediately after the missed call.

    

      "Hey [Name], this is [Your Name] with the Daniel Sa Team at NFM Lending. I just tried to give you a quick call to introduce myself. We love working alongside agents to support their buyers, create listing-specific payment strategies, and explore creative financing options to help you and your clients win in this market. I would love to connect for a quick 20-minute conversation. Do you have any availability next week?"
    


    

      Practice note from leadership: These scripts — along with objection handling and value proposition scenarios — are available in Join Ascend. You have login access. Please practice for 10 minutes before starting calls each day and share your feedback on how it's going.

Daily Schedule

    

      
Your Role

      
Relationship Manager — Daniel Sa Team, NFM Lending

      
Responsible for growing the team's active agent count and keeping those relationships producing. An active agent is one who has sent us a lead — agents who haven't sent a lead within 90 days fall off the active list and move to the Reconnect queue. Two core responsibilities: bring in new agents through cold calling, outreach, and meetings; and retain the agents we have through consistent, value-driven contact, feedback, and follow-through so they keep referring their clients.

    

    
Working from home: The expectation is that you are actively working your full hours, on Teams, and responsive. If something comes up and you are not able to work, please take PTO or come into the office. WFH is a privilege and the standard is the same as being in office.

Encompass Cheat Guide

    
You don't need to know how to process a file. You need to know where to look when you're talking to an agent. Before every call, have three things open: RETR (to reference their recent sales), Encompass (to check file status), and the team inbox (to catch any recent updates).


    
Milestones — what each one means

    

      
StartedApplication has been taken. File is open and still with the sales team.

      
ProcessingFile is with the loan manager for review. Being prepared for submittal to underwriting.

      
SubmittalLoan has been submitted to underwriting for initial review.

      
ApprovalUnderwriting has reviewed and approved. There may still be conditions to satisfy — check the Conditions tab for what's outstanding.

      
ResubSubmitted to underwriting for final review. Normal and expected — doesn't mean something went wrong. If an agent asks why, tell them it's a standard step in the review process.

      
Clear to Close (CTC)All conditions met. Ready to schedule closing. Your trigger for a CTC call to both agents.

      
Received by CloserClosing docs are with the title/closing company. Closing is imminent.

      
Docs SigningClosing docs are out to title. Borrower is signing.

    


    
Conditions — what they mean

    

      
Prior to DocsMust be satisfied before closing docs can be sent. File cannot move forward until these are cleared.

      
Prior to FundingMust be cleared before funds are released. Closing can happen but money doesn't move until these are done.

    


    
Rate lock

    
Find the lock status and expiration date in the loan file under Rate Lock or Pricing. If a lock is expiring soon and the file isn't close to closing, flag it to the LP. Rate conversations stay with the LP team.


    
Where to find things mid-call

    

      
Current milestonePipeline view — the status column next to the file. Check before every call.

      
Closing dateTop of the loan file in property and closing details. Check if it matches the contract date — if they don't align, mention it to the LP.

      
Outstanding conditionsConditions tab. Shows what's still needed and whether it's prior to docs or prior to funding.

      
Assigned LP / ProcessorFile summary under team members. If you need to escalate, this tells you who to go to.

      
Buyer infoBorrower summary tab — name, contact, employment, loan amount.

      
Rate lock statusRate Lock or Pricing section. Locked vs. floating, and expiration date.

    


    
Attend the weekly pipeline call every Tuesday at 10:30am. This is where you'll hear what's moving, what's stalled, and what agents may need a touch on. It's one of the best ways to stay ahead of conversations before agents call you.

    
If an agent asks something you can't answer with confidence, it's always fine to say "Let me look into that and get back to you shortly" — then actually follow up. Never guess on a file detail.


    
  






  

    
Resources

    
These tools exist to help you find answers quickly and show up prepared. The more you use them, the more confident you'll sound in the field.


    

      
RETRAgent and brokerage lookup — and your secret weapon before every cold call and reconnect. Pull up their profile before you dial: see their recent sales, current listings, and brokerage. Congratulating them on a specific recent sale ("I saw your listing at 123 Main just closed — congrats!") turns a cold call into a warm one instantly.

      
NFM ChatGPTGreat for drafting, quick explanations, and thinking through how to handle a conversation. Use it before, during, or after calls.

      
All RegsNFM's underwriting guidelines. A solid resource when you want to understand what's possible for a buyer before the conversation goes further.

      
EncompassFile status, milestones, conditions, closing dates. Have it open before agent calls. See the Encompass Cheat Guide for where to find specific things.

      
Team inboxHave it open and searchable before calls. If an agent asks about something specific on a file, you can pull the relevant email thread in real time in real time.

      
VelocifyCRM where leads are added and tracked. When an agent refers a buyer, this is where the lead lives. Have it open so you can see what's been referred and by whom.

    


    
The more familiar these tools become, the more confident you'll sound in the field. When you do need to bring something to the team, coming in with what you already found makes the conversation faster and makes the conversation more productive for everyone.
